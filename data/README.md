# Data sources

Every input used in this study, and how to obtain it.

---

## 1. SARS-CoV-2 sequences — GISAID (NOT redistributable)

Spike protein sequences and whole genomes were downloaded from the **GISAID EpiCoV**
database (<https://gisaid.org>) for strains collected between **1 November 2021 and
30 November 2022**.

GISAID's terms of use prohibit redistribution, so **no sequence or metadata file from
GISAID appears in this repository**.

Anyone with a free GISAID account can retrieve precisely these records from that
identifier. We gratefully acknowledge all originating and submitting laboratories; the
full acknowledgement table is provided as a supplementary table of the paper.

Two separate sequence sets were used:

| Set | Use | Size after filtering |
|---|---|---|
| Spike protein sequences | RBD extraction → trait prediction | 1,121 distinct RBD sequences |
| Whole genomes, 9 countries | Time-scaled phylogenies | 533–1,193 per country |

Filters applied to the spike set: >5% ambiguous residues removed; length outside
1,263–1,273 residues removed; records lacking a collection date or lineage label removed.
RBD was taken as spike positions 331–531 after alignment to Wuhan-Hu-1 with Clustal Omega.

---

## 2. Global anchor sequences — GenBank and Nextstrain

338–376 sequences per country, collected December 2019 – December 2022, subsampled from a
global Nextstrain dataset and excluding the target country.

- GenBank: <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/>
- Nextstrain: <https://nextstrain.org>
- Reference genome Wuhan-Hu-1: `NC_045512.2`

---

## 3. ACE2-binding deep mutational scanning

Binding measurements for all combinations of 15 RBD mutations in the Omicron BA.1
background (32,565 genotypes), filtered to variants carrying 1–5 mutations, leaving
**4,923 measurements**.

- Originally generated by **Moulana et al. (2022)**, *Nature Communications*
  <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34506-z>
- Obtained from the repository of **Wang et al. (2024)**, *PNAS*
  <https://github.com/Dianzhuo-Wang/COVID19-Biophysical-Model>
  (paper: <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2314518121>)

The ESM-1v representation of each mutant RBD sequence was also taken from that pipeline
rather than re-implemented here; see [`../code/01_data_prep/README.md`](../code/01_data_prep/README.md).

Place the downloaded files at `data/external/`.

---

## 4. Antibody escape

Antibody binding retained was computed with the antibody-escape estimator of
**Greaney et al. (2022)**, *Virus Evolution* <https://doi.org/10.1093/ve/veac021>.

Their `escapecalculator.py` is **not redistributed here**. Obtain it from the authors'
repository and place it in `code/03_traits/` before running that stage:

- Code: <https://github.com/jbloomlab/SARS2-RBD-escape-calc>
- Interactive version: <https://jbloomlab.github.io/SARS2-RBD-escape-calc/>

---

## 5. Lineage growth rates, cell-entry score and socio-demographic index

Adopted unchanged from our previous work:

- Ding, Z. and Yuan, H.-Y. (2026) *iScience* <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.114979>
- Ding, Z. and Yuan, H.-Y. (2026) *International Journal of Infectious Diseases*
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijid.2025.108260>

The lineage-level values actually used here are included in
`data/processed/merged_binding_final_updated.csv`.

---

## 6. ESM-1v model weights

Pre-trained weights are downloaded automatically by `fair-esm`
(<https://github.com/facebookresearch/esm>); no ACE2-binding, immune-escape or fitness
labels were used in pre-training. Model: `esm1v_t33_650M_UR90S_1`.
